It took several months for me to get to level 60. On top of that, I was leveling up in a terrible spot. The enemies on the map were weak when I got to level 50 and I wasn’t getting many experience points (XP) after each battle. To be honest, I was stuck there. I had no idea what to do to advance the plot and unlock new sections of the map, where I would find stronger enemies with more XP. After more than 60 hours of gameplay (which was a lot in the early 2000s) I gave up and stopped playing. Years later, I gave this game, Legend of Legaia, a retry. This time, I was able to finish it. From there, I set more ambitious goals such as getting all party members to level 99, discovering all the secret fighting techniques, and getting all magic powers to level 9. By the time I achieved all my goals, I had spent hundreds of hours doing what could be seen as nothing: pressing buttons in sequence and watching the same cutscenes over and over again while numbers on the screen increased with each cycle. What was the point of that?

Little did I know back then, but I was working out, stretching my mental muscles toward performance and endurance, developing life skills useful for professional and personal development. No matter whether in the fictional world of medieval times, in the gym, or in an office building, there is something powerful and unique about working towards a large vision fueled by passionate and incessant effort. In the gaming community, this fuel is called grind.

When grinding, players perform the same set of tasks many times over the course of hours, days, weeks, months, or even years, all to achieve a goal such as maxing out the stats screen numbers, finishing the game as fast as possible or without taking any damage. The skills required to do some of the hardest tasks in video games may take years to develop. As they are practicing, players develop a set of emotional skills akin to those of professional athletes, such as patience, resilience, acceptance and working under pressure.

Grinding emotions

Video games were created primarily for entertainment. [1] Whether alone or with other people, players engage with a virtual world through avatars, overcoming challenges and achieving objectives while enjoying the experience. Over the years, a number of researchers demonstrated that video-games can also be used to develop skills. [1] Many game-like programs were created, blending the experience of playing games with pedagogical practices. As a result, you can play a math or language game, for instance, and learn by immersing yourself in a world crafted to help you develop these skills. Many public and private schools in countries such as the United States or Brazil make use of learning games as part of their K-12 curriculum.

Learning with the aid of video games is not a new concept, but only recently have researchers started making the connection between playing games and developing emotional skills. [1] Less than 20 years ago, universities across the United States began granting scholarships to exceptional players of e-sports, on the basis that professional players (pro-players) are athletes. [2] In e-sports, players belong to teams and face other teams in a virtual arena. Some of the most popular e-sports are first person shooters (FPS), in which the goal is to eliminate all opponents from the arena, and multiplayer online battle arena (MOBA), where teams aim to level up and destroy their opponents’ resources. During a match, players must input dozens of commands on the controller in seconds and answer to several signals on the screen to keep up with everything that’s happening, all while working together with the team and managing the pressure of a high-stakes prize tournament being watched by millions of people worldwide. Developing the skills to become a pro-player takes years of practice, challenging players to build resilience and manage their emotions.

Gamers aren’t the only ones familiar with the idea of achieving goals through constant and repetitive practice. Researcher Angela Duckworth coined the term grit to refer to the same concept of achieving goals through constant practice over a long period of time. [3] Her research delves into the question of why some people go to great lengths to achieve goals. Having spoken with dozens of athletes and business people, she was able to both understand the nature of grit and devise means to foster it from a young age. In her book, Grit, she suggests parents practice with their children what she calls the Hard Thing Rule: the whole family should choose and commit to doing something hard every day over a minimum period of time. Each family member get to choose what he or she wants to do, like learning a language, an instrument or starting a business. They must practice every day, and they must commit with their chosen hard thing for at least two years. Duckworth argues that this routine develops the necessary traits to build resilience when pursuing goals, even if at the end of the established period they decide to do something else instead. Another finding from Duckworth’s research is what she calls deliberate practice: a systematic approach in which top performers constantly challenge themselves and measure their efforts to improve their skills.

Leveling up for success

Many games that weren’t originally designed with pedagogical objectives in mind can be repurposed to develop emotional skills through grinding. By setting goals, charting plans and performing deliberate practice, players can find new ways to play their favorite games while building up skills that will accompany them throughout their lives.

To illustrate the concept of building emotional skills through video-games, I’ll introduce you to Legend of Legaia, one of my favorite childhood games. Released in Japan in 1998, and in North America in 1999, this role-playing game (RPG) tells the story of a post-apocalytic world. Humans coexist with creatures known as Seru. Seru were benign creatures, but became hostile once a mysterios cloud called Mist enveloped the world ten years prior to the events of the game. Humans are no match by the powers of the Seru, and they built fortresses and fortified buildings to defend themselves. You take control of a group of three characters (a party), and your goal is to travel the world getting rid of the Mist, saving humanity from extinction. As you travel and defeat the Seru, your party becomes stronger, being able to battle against stronger opponents and dungeon bosses.

I’ll walk you through my process to achieve the maximum level for all party members, which is 99, starting from level 1.

Consistency and incremental progress

The simulated worlds of video-games are limited. They represent a subset of the physical world, providing an environment that’s similar to any tabletop game. Even open-world games with seamlessly never ending maps or full of quests and side quests are bound to the constraints set by game developers. Your avatar can interact with certain objects and the environment in a limited set of ways. This makes it easier to focus on the task at hand, and plan your moves within the boundaries of the game, creating a space suitable for deliberate practice.

Let’s see how to make this space. Legend of Legaia is a turn-based game. Battles are done in turns. You plan your move in advance and the round begins. You make your move and your opponent makes theirs. At the end of the round, you plan your next move, and a new round starts. The cycle repeats itself until the battle is over. If you win, you earn XP and, occasionally, items that can be used to enhance your party or sold at shops. A single battle can last several minutes, and stronger foes reward you with more XP. You find enemies at random by walking around dungeons or the open-world.

At the beginning of the game, you start at level 1, facing foes that are as strong as you. You have to battle them to advance your level and face stronger opponents. After a few battles, you’ll realize that you need more and more XP to get to the next level, requiring you to win even more battles. You should then find the best possible place to earn XP, so you can achieve level 99 faster (unless your goal is to achieve level 99 with additional constraints. [4]). This will require you to advance the plot, unlocking stronger foes and better equipment, including accessories that will double the amount of XP you earn after each battle. For a new player, only getting to the optimal spot wearing the optimal equipment without guidance can easily take more than 30 hours. If you manage to do that without getting lost or doing too many extra battles in non-optimal places, you should get to the spot with one third of the goal already accomplished. Once you get there, the real work can begin.

Discipline and patience

In his book Be Useful, Arnold Schwarzenegger explains that building skill takes not only time but a lot of “boring” practice. [5] He illustrates this by describing the life of the late jazz saxophonist John Coltrane. Coltrane was able to perform some of the best improvisations in the world of jazz on stage, but during every practice session, he played the same basic scales over and over again. Schwarzenegger attributes his success to constantly practicing basic scales.

Constant practice makes the bulk of achievement, and it is no different with our endeavor of getting to the maximum level for the entire party in Legend of Legaia. It is time to find the best possible spot for deliberate practice. This isn’t simply the place where strongest enemies lie. You should take other variables into account, such as the time and effort to win battles. The strongest enemies will take longer to be defeated than at the next best place to earn XP. Defeating enemies in the second-best spot will reward you with 2.244 XP less than what you could get every two battles. The tradeoff is receiving a little less XP every two battles, but winning more battles in the same amount of time to make more XP in the long run.

Enemies in the Bio Castle, the final dungeon of the game, will spawn in pairs or trios, and for each battle, the party will encounter two or three types of Seru. The order in which these Seru appear is not random: every battle will put you up against one type of Seru, rotating them in the same order. In the last room, before the final boss, you can find the enemies that drop the most XP after being defeated: Pueras and Gilliums. Each time a battle begins, you’ll find three Pueras or three Gilliums. Defeating these will reward the party with 27.723 XP (without Crimson Books, accessories that double the XP of the wearer after each battle).

Meanwhile, there is a room in the Bio Castle in which the party finds several people glued to the walls and floor of the room. (the Bio Castle, as the name implies, is made of organic matter, and near the end of the game, it fused with the people that were close to it as it was growing.) In this room, you’ll find three Spoons and three Barras. Once defeated, the party will be rewarded 25.479 XP without Crimson Books. Enemies in this room, though, are way easier to defeat than those in the final room, making this place the best to grind.

To put these numbers into perspective, the weakest enemy, the Green Slime, rewards you with 32 XP. To achieve level 99 with a single character in your party, you’ll need approximately 10 million XP. To get there, you’ll certainly encounter many obstacles along the way.

Overcoming challenges

You have a lot of battles ahead. Prepare yourself to spend countless hours listening to sinister music, running the same sequence of commands during each battle, and waiting all the animations to play over. You should have a lot of magic power (MP) and health power (HP) recovery items to stay in the Bio Castle for as long as possible. After a while, you’ll run out of supplies and will have to retreat to buy more in shops. Luckily, that won’t be a problem, because as you win battles, you earn money. If you buy as much recovery items as possible, you’ll be able to grind for a long time. Not being careful with your inventory may kill you, forcing you to start from your last save point. There are no auto-saves as you progress. You need to save your game using a save statue, which is a magic platform that you can find inside dungeons, villages or other areas without enemies. Not saving your game and managing your items can make you lose progress in a painful way.

Additionally, there are many enemies and many possible ways to fight them. Take Gilliums for example. These Seru are very resistant to both physical and magical attacks. Seru creatures have affinity with one element, such as water, ice or fire. Gilliums have affinity with thunder, like Gala (one of the party characters). Every time Gala attacks a Gillium, he’ll only be able to do half the damage he would normally be able to. [6] Every Seru in Bio Castle has affinity with one element and most of them have a weakness against another one. This is useful to plan your attacks and make optimal use of your resources.

Following your plan to heart and addressing eventual unforseen events doesn’t guarantee success, but keeps you on a steady path to achieve it.

Achievement and motivation

As you grind, your mind will begin to work in “auto-pilot” mode, and you may feel anxious about the time that takes to reach a single level once you’ve advanced enough. This is when you have to devise strategies to keep your emotions in check, such as visualizing yourself having achieved your goal, and remembering what that means for you and for the people that are rooting for your success. Additionally, by doing deliberate practice you should always be conscious of the entire grinding process, optimizing your strategy to maximize the outcome of each battle. You have to keep in mind that level requirements increase exponentially, but the amount of XP you can collect after battles is fixed. This may lead to the false impression that your efforts count less and less towards the goal. Remember that you’ll have to achieve around 10 million XP with each character in your party anyway, no matter how level upgrade requirements increase. This is somehow similar to investing in assets with fixed rates. At the end of the investment period, you’ll have you money back with the promised interest, but at any given day you’ll notice that your assets will value more or less than what you bought them for. If you only pay attention to the current price of your assets instead of the agreement for the long run, you will get to the false conclusion that you’re losing or making more money than what you estimated when purchasing the assets. In other words: you have to see it through the end and deal with whatever cards life gave you.

To wrap this playthrough, don’t forget to recognize and commemorate achievements and milestones. A great way to do that is to check the stats screen after every ten levels to see how much you have improved your party. You’re climbing a mountain. Look down to see how much you’ve accomplished.

Grinding IRL

Stretching the boundaries of video games can bring joy and accomplishment in ways game developers have never envisioned, with practical consequences in real life (IRL). Games Done Quick (GDQ) is an event held in the United States twice a year, in which pro-players beat several games as fast as possible while live-streaming to thousands of people worldwide. [7] These events, called charity marathons, aim to raise money to help non-profit organizations such as the Prevent Cancer Foundation and Doctors Without Borders research cures for diseases.

For individuals interested in the relationship between the digital world of video games and the development of emotional skills, give your favorite game a try with a fresh set of eyes. If you’ve never been into video-games and don’t have a favorite title yet, explore the game shelves and see what is out there, just like you explore the shelves of books when in a library. You may not like what you find at first, but that’s ok. I heard it once that finding the right book is like dating: you may not get it right the first time. I think this is the same with games. What’s important is that you don’t stop looking for what resonates with you. Once you find it, stick with it. Remember the Hard Thing Rule: set a hard goal and work towards it, giving your best each time you play, measuring your progress and keeping consistency. This will build and strenghten the skills that you’ll need to achieve whatever goals you set yourself to achieve.
